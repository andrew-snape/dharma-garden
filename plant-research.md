# Plant research: Mount Duneed / Geelong 3217

Research notes for new plants in the garden game. Verified where a source is named; anything else is flagged in "Uncertain or unverified".

## 1. Growing conditions summary

Mount Duneed sits on the Victorian volcanic plains south-west of Geelong, with clay-loam soils that are nutrient rich and crack in summer. The climate is temperate: cool wet-ish winters with frosts, and warm dry summers. Frosts are most likely from roughly June to August (approximate; the Bureau of Meteorology table for Mount Duneed itself could not be retrieved). The nearest long-term station is Geelong Airport (Grovedale), where mean July minimum is about 5.4 C, which points to frost-prone winters. A local weather-watcher report for the 2026 winter recorded three frost days in July (lowest -1.4 C) and one in August. Rainfall is fairly even through the cooler months, with a drier late winter/early spring in 2026. Summers (roughly December to February) are hot and dry, and the Grovedale mean for January is about 37 mm. Sources: [Exploroz, Duneed / Grovedale station](https://exploroz.com/places/54640/vic+duneed), [Geelong Indy, winter weather report](https://geelongindy.com.au/?p=34587). Treat these as indicative only, not a full climate normal.

## 3. Recommended starter additions

1. **Chocolate lily (native flower):** A spring lily native to the Victorian region, frost tolerant and low water once established, so it suits a dry-tolerant mechanic. Its lily shape is simple to draw as a flat stem with a few petals.
2. **Common everlasting (native flower):** A yellow button-daisy groundcover that needs very little water, which gives a clear low-water contrast to the taller flowers already in the game.
3. **Calendula (exotic flower):** Orange daisy-like flower on a single stem, with frost tolerance and a relatively quick cycle (around 8 weeks), so it fills the frost-hardy, quick-bloom slot.
4. **Cosmos (exotic flower):** A pink or white daisy on a tall stem, faster to bloom than many annuals (around 2 months) and frost-tender, so it adds a summer-only heat mechanic.
5. **Basil (herb):** About 4 weeks to harvest with a simple green oval leaf, the quickest harvest in the set and frost-tender.
6. **Zucchini (vegetable):** A large yellow trumpet flower with a harvestable fruit, a 2 to 3 month cycle, and a heat-loving mechanic, which gives a clear contrast with basil.

Colour and growth-time spread across the six: yellow, orange, pink, green (basil), and yellow again (zucchini). Growth times run from about 4 weeks (basil) to 3 months (zucchini), with the native lily and everlasting at longer, unverified cycles.

## 5. Uncertain or unverified

- **Mount Duneed frost and rainfall:** Bureau of Meteorology tables for Mount Duneed or Geelong could not be retrieved. Frost months (June to August) and rainfall figures are indicative, based on a neighbouring station and a local weather-watcher report. The Breakwater station's rainfall may be unreliable for August 2026.
- **Geelong local status:** Chocolate lily and common everlasting are confirmed on Geelong lists (Recreating the Country; Zone 1 sheet). Billy buttons and bulbine lily are Victorian natives, but I could not confirm they appear on the Geelong or Mount Duneed zone lists. The Zone 8 (Mount Duneed) PDF could not be read, so its species list is unchecked.
- **Native flowering months:** Sources conflict. Common everlasting is listed as July to October, September to December, and summer. Bulbine lily and billy buttons also vary by source (roughly spring into summer, or September to January).
- **Native time to first bloom:** No source gave weeks from sowing to bloom for any native. Those cells are blank ("Not verified").
- **Chocolate lily flower colour:** Not confirmed in the sources I read.
- **Chocolate lily edibility:** Sources conflict, so no edible claim is made.
- **Native sarsaparilla frost tolerance:** Gardening with Angus says not frost tolerant; Austplants and the Sunshine Coast Botanic Garden say hardy once established. Young plants may need protection.
- **Exotic flowers:** Calendula and cosmos timings come from a non-Australian site (Home and Gardens). Diggers Club and Costa sources were not retrieved. Zinnia timing comes from one Australian garden site. Victorian sowing months for all three are unverified.
- **Vegetable planting months:** Harvest times come from Yarra Ranges (cooler hills). The planting-month calendar did not load, so sowing months are unverified for Geelong.
- **Blue flax-lily, pink bells, kangaroo grass:** Not researched in this pass. Kangaroo grass is a grass, not a flower.
- **Sowing windows:** from a general Cool Climate chart that includes Geelong, not Mount Duneed specifically. The chart gives no days to harvest. "Viola" may not be the native *Viola hederacea*, so confirm the species before using it.

## 7. Sowing information notes (The Seed Collection)

Source: [Understanding our Sowing Information](https://www.theseedcollection.com.au/Understanding-our-Sowing-Information). The page calls its information general and says it does not account for varieties, climates or situations.

- **Germination:** 1 to 4 weeks, depending on variety and weather. Keep the soil moist, never wet or dry.
- **Days to harvest:** the page defines this as days until the plant reaches its usable stage (fruit, flower, or leaves ready to pick). It is the measure the game's bloom and harvest timings should follow.
- **Frost hardiness:** frost tender (killed by frost), half hardy (survives some frost, not a full winter), or hardy (survives winter). Several of the plants above are frost tender: basil, zucchini, cosmos, zinnia.
- **Position:** full sun is 6 hours or more a day, part sun 3 to 6 hours, shade under 3 hours.
- **Life cycle:** annual (one year), biennial (two years), or perennial (more than two years). Chives and native violet are perennial. Carrot is biennial but grown as an annual. The others here are annual.
- **Australian seasons:** autumn is March to May, winter June to August, spring September to November, summer December to February.
- **Geelong's climate zone** on the page is "Cool" (hot, dry summers, cold winters, some frosts).
